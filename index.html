What you should notice

  • Latency: Neural decoding and stimulation loops run slower than spinal reflex arcs; the prosthetic “chases” the target.
  • Noise & drift: Brain signals are noisy; small unintentional movements appear as jitter or wavering.
  • Speed cap: Motors and safety limits prevent whip-fast motions even when your intent is sudden.
  • Coarser control: Few effective degrees of freedom vs. a biological hand/wrist; motion can feel “filtered.”
  • Developmental history: Use the toggle above the canvas—same prosthetic model, but the “congenital” profile adds slower filtering, more jitter, coarser steps, and corrective “hunting,” loosely inspired by differences reported between congenital and acquired limb-loss cohorts.

How this maps to real BCIs

Real implanted or non-invasive BCIs add calibration sessions, occasional remapping, fatigue effects, and shared autonomy (robot helps with grasp timing). This demo exaggerates constraints so differences are visible in a few seconds—clinical systems vary widely by user, implant, and task.
